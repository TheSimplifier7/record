How it is worked out

Last close is the last Friday close in record.json before the level was named. A floor holds when the week closes at or above it; a ceiling holds when the week closes at or under it. A floor above the last close, or a ceiling under it, asked price to move. The others gave price room.

Random week held is the share of week-on-week changes in record.json's closes, for the same metal, that would have left the week on the level's side if it had been named that far from the close: 19 weeks for gold, 17 for silver and 10 for platinum. The figure from a random week is the sum of those shares.

What it does not show

18 levels is too few to prove an edge either way. The weeks used for the base rate are the same weeks the levels were graded in, so a falling market makes ceilings look easy for everyone; platinum's high shares are that. 29 of the 47 graded rows are left out, because they carry no stated side or were named before the first close on file, and giving them a side now would be a change to the record.
