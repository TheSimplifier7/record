The one who publishes
his wrong calls.

Ten years on an institutional gold desk. What he runs now is a public ledger where every call is named in advance, the exact condition that would prove it wrong is published beside it, and it is graded at Friday's close. The wrong ones stay up, and nothing has ever been deleted.

The short version

A decade on an institutional gold desk in London, trading his own capital since 2003, now based in Spain.

The method splits capital into three books by timeframe, ranked one way, with the fastest book the smallest and the last to open. It is the opposite of how this industry usually sells trading.

Every call it produces is published in advance with the condition that would kill it, and graded in public on the close. That ledger is the only thing he asks anyone to judge him on.

The record, which is the whole point

Public, dated, and never edited

Three segments he can carry

  1. The calls that were wrong, and why the kill condition mattered more than the call. Nobody in this sector publishes what would prove them wrong. He walks through the ones that fired, with their kills and their closes.
  2. What an institutional gold desk does that retail never sees. Ten years of it, told as process rather than nostalgia.
  3. Why the fastest book is the smallest and opens last. The arithmetic most traders never run: a short holding period keeps a fraction of the move, carries most of the noise, and pays the full spread either way. The spread does not get smaller because you were quick.

Scope, so nobody has to ask

Education only. He is not an authorised or regulated investment adviser in any jurisdiction. He does not give price targets, does not name trades, and does not give a view on any live position, on air or anywhere else. He discusses method, process, and calls that are already published and already graded. Producers get the benefit of a guest who cannot say anything that creates a problem for the show.
