The bank board

Every dated gold, silver and platinum price target the big banks publish, graded on the weekly close when its deadline arrives. The wrong ones stay.

A bank publishes a price target with a deadline and nothing that could prove it wrong. When the deadline passes, the target is revised and the first number is gone. This board keeps the first number. Each row is the exact quote with its date and source, what would prove it wrong under the one rule below, and the weekly close that decided it. A revised target is a new row; the old one stays and is graded on its own deadline. None of this counts in my own record. These are not my calls. My own wrong calls are here, and they were on the page first.

In plain words: a target is held if price closes a week at the target or beyond it before its deadline. If the deadline passes without that close, it is wrong.

The one rule. Held if a weekly close on the target's side of it, at or above for a target above price, at or below for a target below, prints on or before the last weekly close inside the window. Wrong if the window closes without one. A range is graded on its nearer bound. Graded on the TVC weekly close, read off this desk's own chart, the same close every row on the record is graded on; where a close predates this desk's own reads the row says as reported and the figure is replaced when it is read, and a grade never turns on the decimals. A bank's target is usually read as a print on any day; this page grades closes and nothing else, and says so here so nobody is surprised.

Not on the board, and why

    A claim with no window cannot be graded and is not a target; it is a view. Averages for a year cannot be graded on a close. "Toward", "approach" and "could" are not levels. The board leaves them out by name so the leaving out is itself on the record.

    How a row gets here

    The desk reads the wires and the banks' own research notes as they are reported, on a fixed day each week, and takes a row only when it has an institution, one direction, a level, and a deadline that falls on a date. The quote is copied, never paraphrased. When the last weekly close before the deadline prints, the row is graded on that close and the result is written beside it, and it does not move again. Seen a dated target that is not here? The desk takes them by message, with the link. One rule, whoever published it.
