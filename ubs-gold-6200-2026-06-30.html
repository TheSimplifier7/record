The kill this desk assigned

Held if a weekly close at or above 6,200 printed on or before the last weekly close of June 2026; wrong if the window closed without one.

The target was published without one. The same rule grades every row on the board, and this desk's own levels on the record.

Wrong. The window's last weekly close was Friday 26 June 2026, 4,088.97 read off the founder's chart, 2,111.03 under the line. No weekly close at or above 6,200 printed inside the window: gold's high of 2026 is 5,589.38 on 28 January, as reported. The grade does not turn on the decimals.

The backstory

Gold printed its high of 2026 on 28 January, 5,589.38 as reported. The next day UBS raised its gold target to 6,200 for March, June and September 2026, up from 5,000. One number, three windows, one sentence, so the board grades it as three rows, each on its own window.

June was the second window. It shut on the 26 June close at 4,088.97, read off the founder's chart, 2,111.03 under the line. Wrong, and it stays.

A month before it shut, on 27 May, UBS cut its year-end target from 5,900 to 5,500. That cut is its own row.

How every row is graded

The one rule. Held if a weekly close on the target's side of it, at or above for a target above price, at or below for a target below, prints on or before the last weekly close inside the window. Wrong if the window closes without one. A range is graded on its nearer bound. Graded on the TVC weekly close, read off this desk's own chart, the same close every row on the record is graded on; where a close predates this desk's own reads the row says as reported, and a grade never turns on the decimals.
