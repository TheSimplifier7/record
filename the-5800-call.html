The $5,800 Call

In February a bank said gold would hit $5,800 an ounce in the second quarter. It came with no way to be proved wrong. Here is the one Friday close that would have settled it, and what gold did.

THE SIMPLIFIER · 7 October 2026 · Every close read off the founder's own chart

16 February 2026

Kitco ran it under a plain headline: ANZ sees gold hitting $5,800 an ounce in the second quarter.

“In their latest note on gold, commodity analysts at ANZ said they expect prices to hit $5,800 an ounce in the second quarter, up sharply from their previous target of $5,400 an ounce.”

Kitco News, 16 February 2026

The same article had gold consolidating around $5,000. The new number sat about $800 above the price, and $400 above the bank's own last one.

What holds

Read the date before the number. Ask what price did in the weeks before a target was set.

Three weeks earlier

Gold had already printed its high of the year, in the last week of January.

5,598.75

That is the high on the founder's weekly chart. Kitco's piece said as much: “Gold's sharp drop from last month's record highs near $5,600 has some investors worried.”

So the call needed gold to climb back through a high it had just turned down from, and then 201.25 further.

The test it came without

A forecast is worth reading when something can prove it wrong. This one gave a level and a quarter, and no close that would settle it.

So it gets the test every target on the record gets. It holds if one Friday close at or above 5,800 prints on or before the last Friday close of the window. It is wrong if the window shuts without one.

It is a generous test. Every Friday from 16 February to the end of June counts, and one close is enough.

What holds

If a call can't be wrong, it can't be graded. Ask for the level, the date and the close that settles it.

What holds

One close is a low bar. A call that can't clear it in four months was wrong, whatever came after.

The board

Fifteen targets, one rule, and one date that splits them

The record keeps a board of published bank targets, each graded by the rule ANZ just met. Fourteen are for gold. Line them up by the day they were published and they fall on either side of 28 January, the day gold printed its 2026 high.

What holds

Before the January high, three gold targets on the board came due. All three held. After it, four came due. All four were wrong. Seven are still open.

The three that held were all at 5,000, below the high gold went on to print. The four that failed were all above it. Seven graded rows is a small sample, and the open ones may yet hold. It is a pattern on one board, worth holding up against the next target you read.

Silver: Citi's 100 by March, published 13 January, held. It is left off the gold scale. The 2026 high on this chart is the founder's weekly read, 5,598.75; Kitco reported it as 5,589.38 on 28 January.

Method

The rule: a target holds if a weekly close on its side prints on or before the last weekly close inside its window. It is wrong if the window shuts without one. A range is graded on its nearer bound.

Nothing on this page is a forecast. Every figure is a past close or a published claim.
