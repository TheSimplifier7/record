Held the close stayed on the right side of the named line. Hit it went through the room named with the call as well; a hit counts once, as held, and is marked. Wrong the close crossed the line; the row stays, in red, forever. Never reached price never came to the line, so the week proves nothing and counts for neither side. Open the level is named and the clock is still running, so it is counted for neither side until the close that grades it. Kill the close, named with the call, that would grade it wrong. Side, room and odds every row from 28 September says which way it reads, slate above the line or copper below it, the next level on that side, and the chance the line holds, as printed the day it was named.

The direction, and what would end it

In plain words: the week-to-week call was retired on 20 September with the line it was drawn on. Its own kill had not fired at its last read, so it ended neither held nor wrong, and it stays below as it stood. The year-to-year call has stood since 7 June and names the cross that ends it.

Graded on their own clocks, never counted in the tally above. The desk's note on the clocks, and what turned when

Graded on their own clocks against a named close, never restamped, and never counted in the tally above. When one ends it stays on this page with the date it ended and what ended it, and when one metal inside a two-metal call ends, the end is written into the call and the call stays. Gold has been bearish since 13 March. That structure ended on the 7 August close, the swing clock turned bullish the same night, and it is bullish still. Silver turned bullish on the 21 August close and closed under its line on 28 August without the structure turning, so the claim stands: from 29 August this page dates a swing turn by the epoch midline crossing the slow mean reversion line, never by a price close, which is the standard the secular clock has run on since 1 August. The weekly rows keep their own published price kills and are graded on them, which is why the silver row of 23 August grades wrong and stays. Platinum's first row on this page passed at the same close and on the same rule, and its swing clock runs from the 19 August close. See notes 06, 08, 10 and 11.

Graded from the crowd

The banks' targets and the loud accounts' claims, quoted exactly with their date and their source, given the kill they were published without, and graded on the close when the window shuts. Nothing removed when the number is revised. The claim, never the person. None of it is in this desk's tally.

The banks' targets

PublishedSourceTargetWindowAs publishedGrade

One rule. Held if a weekly close on the target's side of it prints on or before the last weekly close inside the window; wrong if the window closes without one. A range is graded on its nearer bound. Averages, "toward", "approach" and claims with no window are not targets and are not on the board; they are listed by name, with the reason, on the full board, with every quote. Every close on this board is read off this desk's own chart; where one is not yet, the row says as reported and the figure is replaced when it is read. A grade never turns on the decimals.
