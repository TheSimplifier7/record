What was written about it afterwards
Note 02 · Standing calls, added 31 July 2026.

The directional position runs on a longer clock than a level and resolves on a different question, so it is stated separately and graded separately. Standing calls never enter the tally: averaging a call that runs for months with one that resolves on a Friday would flatter both. Each carries the close that ends it, and every figure beside it carries the date it is measured from. When one ends it stays, with the date and what ended it.

The standing week-to-week call is restated, not ended, and on 29 August it was restated a second time by a ruling that reached it and not the rows. THE SWING CLOCK TURNS ON A CROSS, NOT A PRICE CLOSE: what dates a turn is the epoch midline crossing the slow mean reversion line, and position against the line is the confirmation, never the trigger. That is not a new idea on this page, it is the standard already applied to the secular clock since 1 August under note 06, extended by the founder to the swing clock and read on his own chart on 29 August. Under it, silver's swing claim did not end on the 28 August close. Silver closed 0.29 under its line and the structure never turned, so the standing direction stands in all three metals. THE RULING DOES NOT REACH THE ROW, and the distinction is the reason this note is long. The weekly rows are a different object on a different clock: each names a level before the week and publishes a price close as the kill, and each is graded on exactly what it published. The silver row of 23 August named a weekly close back below 66.66 as its kill, the close printed 66.3700, and that kill fired. The row grades WRONG and it stays wrong. Note 01 has said since 3 July that no grade is ever restamped to fit a rulebook written after it, and note 03 is the precedent where this desk applied that in the direction that cost it a win. A desk that re-reads its own rulebook on a Saturday and finds its Friday loss was never a loss has stopped keeping a record. The tally is unchanged by the ruling: eighteen correct, four wrong. What the ruling does change, and this is the process defect worth naming, is that the Sunday rows have been publishing a kill that does not match the method the desk actually trades. The row's kill was honest, published and fired; it was also the wrong question to ask of a swing claim. From the next Sunday call, a row naming swing direction states the event that truly ends it, and a row naming a price level keeps a price kill and says which of the two it is. Gold's mark is 2.57% above the 7 August close, down from 6.00% last week. The entry keeps the status live and keeps its stated date of 21 August because nothing was newly stated tonight. Two disclosures with it. First, the paragraph under the standing calls told every reader that silver continues under its own line. That sentence was written on 7 August and was true then. It stopped being true on the 21 August close and was not moved with the entry that night, so it stood stale for one week. It is corrected tonight and logged rather than tidied. Second, platinum's close and its line read were entered separately from gold's and silver's, off the founder's own chart, because the capture taken at the close had the chart's own label covered. Platinum's swing clock is dated from the 19 August close, read off the same chart and supplied on 29 August; for one day this page carried platinum with a graded row and no dated clock, and said so rather than inventing a start. The grade was never in doubt; the two prints were, and they were read before this note published.
