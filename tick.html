The tick book

Intraday calls on the five-minute clock. Named and posted before the trade, graded on the day's close. The wrong ones stay.

The record grades one level a week on Friday's close. This page grades the tactical clock by the same law: a metal, a side, a level and a kill, written and posted before the trade, and graded on the 23:00 Madrid close of the day the row names. Two timestamps per row that this desk cannot edit, the post that named it and the commit that wrote it. No size, no profit or loss, ever: a row is a claim about price and a close, and nothing else is on it. None of this enters the weekly tally.

The one rule. Held if price traded to the level, no five-minute close printed through the kill before the grading close, and the grading close sits on the row's side of the level. Wrong if price traded to the level and either a five-minute close printed through the kill at any time before the grading close, or the grading close is not on the row's side. Never reached if price never traded to the level. The grading close is the last five-minute close before 23:00 Madrid on the day the row names, taken from the exported bars kept beside this page. A row not graded by the next day's open is graded late and says so on the row.

How a row gets here

Before the trade, the level, the side and the kill are posted on X; the post's timestamp is the naming, and it is the one this desk cannot move. The row is written to ticks.js the same day, before the grading close, and every naming post gets a row whatever price did afterwards, so the feed and the book can be laid side by side. After the day's close the five-minute bars for that metal and that day are exported from the chart and saved beside this page, the row is graded from that file by a script that applies the rule and nothing else, and a second script refuses to let any graded row ship whose bars are not on file or disagree with its grade. Every graded row links its bars. Level, kill, side and the minute named are never edited after the first push; if one was wrong it says so in the result and stays.
